## Design Specificity Verdict

**Category-interchangeable above the fold; genuinely authored below it.**

**LLM assessment:** An HVAC contractor or a bookkeeping SaaS could swap the wordmark and three nouns and ship this by lunch. The eyebrow is literally the category name ("Digital agency"). The H1 names no one — swap "systems" for "pipelines" and it fits any B2B service; the word "website" doesn't appear until 200px lower. Services is three abstract nouns on three unmodified Heroicons, including a **lightning bolt for "AI"** — the single most-used icon in SaaS. About is a corporate mad-lib ("Mission / Approach / Values," "enterprise-grade digital infrastructure") that would paste onto a law firm's site verbatim.

The sharpest finding: **there is no imagery anywhere.** No `public/`, no `<img>`, no `next/image`. A web-design agency's own page contains no evidence it has ever designed a web page — while claiming "No templates, no cookie-cutters."

The exception is real and it matters: the pricing bullets and FAQ answers ("so no inquiry goes cold *while you're in session*," "before they call the next name on their list," "How can there be no setup fee?") are written by someone who has had this conversation on the phone. **The most specific content on the page is the last thing anyone reads, and most of it is behind a closed accordion.**

## Overall Impression

The bones are good and the writing is better than the design. Somebody who understands this buyer wrote the pricing and FAQ; somebody following a template built the top of the page. The single biggest opportunity is **inversion** — promote the specific, earned language that currently sits at pixel 2,500 up to pixel 250, and put proof on the page.

The conversion path is where this actually loses money, and it is unfinished in a way no amount of visual polish fixes.

## What's Working

1. **The pricing and FAQ copy is a genuine competitive asset.** "Missed-call text-back, so no inquiry goes cold *while you're in session*" identifies the buyer as a practitioner without a persona doc. FAQ #1 — "How can there be no setup fee?" — pre-empts the exact suspicion a $0-setup offer creates and answers it with the business model instead of a platitude.

2. **Two-tier restraint with a release valve.** Two plans, identical `$0 setup fee · cancel anytime` meta, one clearly featured, and "Start on Starter and upgrade any month, with no new setup fee" underneath. Most agencies ship three tiers plus a fake enterprise column and manufacture anxiety. This makes the wrong choice cheap to undo — correct for a recurring commitment from a cash-conscious buyer.

3. **Accessibility scaffolding well above marketing-page norm.** Real skip link, `aria-labelledby` on every section, correct `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls` pairs, Escape-to-close with focus return, `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"` paired with the CSS media query, and a `<noscript>` block that un-hides Framer's SSR'd `opacity:0`. That last detail is one almost nobody ships.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The conversion moment is an unstyled third-party iframe with no failure path
**Why it matters:** This is the page's only goal — every pixel above it exists to deliver someone here. `FinalCTA.tsx:52-74` renders a bare 700px `calendly-inline-widget` with `strategy="lazyOnload"`, no skeleton, no `onError`, no styling params, and no alternative contact nearby (nearest is a 20px `mailto:` in the footer). Blocked by an ad blocker or merely slow, the visitor gets a blank white rectangle where booking should be. When it *does* load, it arrives in Calendly's navy under a "POWERED BY CALENDLY" ribbon — the voice the page spent 7,000px building evaporates at the second of commitment. The failure is silent in analytics.
**Fix:** Style via params (`?primary_color=0075de&text_color=1a1a18&hide_gdpr_banner=1&hide_event_type_details=1`). Render a styled skeleton in the 700px box; swap on `Script onLoad`; on `onError` or a 6s timeout show a fallback card with email and phone. Move to `afterInteractive` or intersection-load.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The scrolled nav has no background; the wordmark disappears over the dark pricing card
**Why it matters:** `Nav.tsx:45` uses `bg-white/92`. Tailwind v3's opacity scale has no `92` step, so **no rule is generated** — I verified this directly against the served CSS bundle: neither `bg-white/92` nor `bg-white/90` exists in it, and computed `backgroundColor` is `rgba(0,0,0,0)` at scroll. Only `backdrop-blur` remains. Over the near-black System card the wordmark (`#1a1a18`) and hamburger strokes fall to near-zero contrast — failing exactly where the money decision happens, and making the only mobile nav control invisible.
**My error:** when I swept for invalid opacity steps earlier this session I grepped `Pricing.tsx` only. This one was pre-existing in `Nav.tsx` and my sweep never looked there.
**Fix:** `bg-white/[0.92]`. Add `supports-[not(backdrop-filter:blur(0px))]:bg-white` for an opaque fallback.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] There is no proof of any kind on this page
**Why it matters:** No testimonial, client name, work sample, before/after, count, photo, team, or phone number. The only named human anywhere is inside the Calendly iframe. $299/mo recurring to an unknown company is a trust purchase, and taste doesn't clear that bar — the visitor's live question is *"has this person done this for someone like me?"* This is also the cheapest fix for the specificity verdict: real client work is the one asset a competitor cannot swap a logo into.
**Fix:** One proof point above the fold — a named quote with business type ("— Dana R., massage therapist, Tacoma") or a phone mockup showing the missed-call text arriving. Then a 3-up of real site screenshots between Services and Process. If there are no clients yet, say so and lead with the guarantee; an honest "we're new, here's why that's safe" outperforms an implied track record a skeptic will notice is missing.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P1] The plans can't be compared on the device most visitors use
**Why it matters:** `#pricing` is `grid-cols-1 lg:grid-cols-2`, so below 1024px — mobile *and every tablet* — the cards stack. The visitor reads Starter's five bullets, scrolls a full card height, then hits "Everything in Starter, including full local SEO," a pure recall task. On mobile the featured $299 card sits entirely below the fold behind the $149 one, so the tier you want to sell is seen second. The tell: FAQ #2 is "What's the difference between the two plans?" and its answer expands to **562px** of prose doing a table's job.
**Fix:** Order System first on mobile (`order-first lg:order-none`). Replace "Everything in Starter, including…" with an explicit delta naming the inherited items inline. Move the breakpoint from `lg:` to `md:` so tablets get side-by-side.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Plan CTAs promise a transaction and deliver a scroll
**Why it matters:** "Start with Starter" and "Get the Full System" both point at `#contact`; neither starts anything. The anchor lands **419px above the widget** with `scroll-margin-top: 0` under a 68px fixed nav — so on an 812px phone the primary tap yields a headline and a paragraph, no calendar, and a required second scroll. Label/outcome mismatch is the fastest way to lose a skeptic, and that second scroll is exactly where a distracted mobile user leaves.
**Fix:** Relabel to "Book a call about the System." Add `scroll-margin-top: 5rem` to section ids and anchor CTAs to the widget itself, not the section preamble. Better: deep-link a Calendly prefill so the call starts pre-qualified.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Questions to Consider

1. Notion's aesthetic signals *calm tool for knowledge workers*. Your buyer is losing $200 every time a call goes to voicemail. Would that person trust a page that looks like software more, or less, than one that looks like a person?
2. Your best writing sits at pixel 2,500. Your worst — "Digital agency," "Mission / Approach / Values" — sits at pixel 250. If you deleted About entirely and promoted one pricing bullet into the hero, what would you actually lose?
3. FAQ #8 says the site comes down when the plan ends; the hero says "no lock-in." Which will the visitor feel you were hiding — and what happens to conversion if you put that on the pricing card next to the guarantee instead?
4. You sell a system that answers every inquiry instantly so nobody waits. Your own page's only contact path is a 700px iframe needing a third-party script, plus a 20px `mailto:` in the footer. What does it say that the front desk you sell is better than the front desk you run?
